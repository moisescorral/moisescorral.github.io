THE PREMISE

SOMETHING IMPOSSIBLE HAS HAPPENED

In Campo la Carrera, Samantha has found the life she always wanted.
But a threat is about to destroy it.
And her past is coming with it.

Campo la Carrera is one of the quietest places in CONTINUUM. There, far from the big cities, life is simpler.

Until the impossible happens. Something that should never have come back exists again. And nothing will ever be the same.

Campo
la Carrera

A small village at the farthest edge of the world. A refuge for those who want to get away from CONTINUUM's noise. Here, no one asks where you came from or how long you plan to stay.

But everything is about to change.

03 / THE PROTAGONIST

Samantha
Reed

"In Campo la Carrera no one needs to order others around. That's why I stayed"

Samantha chose Campo la Carrera to live apart from other people's decisions. Observant, stubborn and more vulnerable than she lets anyone see, she'll be the first to understand that what happened is no isolated accident.

To find an answer, she'll have to leave the only place she had begun to feel was hers.

04 / THE CHARACTERS

Six people.
Six ways to hide the truth.

01

Will

The new neighbor in Campo la Carrera. Cheeky, sarcastic and too mysterious to go unnoticed. He arrived in the village with a backpack, a half-told story and too many secrets.

"With Will you never know which is more dangerous: what he tells you, or everything he decides to keep quiet."
02

Naomi

Samantha's best friend and the person she trusts most. Smart, decisive and always ready to protect her own, she knows when to step in and when to stay silent. But even the most loyal friendship can hide things that were never said.

"Naomi is always there when Samantha needs her. The question is whether her friendship is as real as Samantha thinks."
03

Thomas

The Directorate's liaison and the official voice of CONTINUUM in Campo la Carrera. Impeccable, methodical and built to always have an answer, even when the system doesn't. But when certainties start to fail, Thomas will have to decide whether to keep obeying or learn to think for himself.

"Thomas was built to follow the Directorate's orders. When everything fails, he can't afford to do the same."
04

Benjamin

The first to help and the last to ask questions. His home has always been open to anyone who needed it, and everyone considers him the best possible neighbor. Almost everyone. Because some people have their reasons to distrust him, and even the best-kept secrets eventually come to light.

"Benjamin is always ready to open his door to you. What no one knows is what he keeps hidden behind it."
05

Cinthya

The authority Campo la Carrera never officially named. Firm, watchful and always ready to step in, she's spent far too long waiting for something to go wrong. She doesn't need to keep secrets of her own: she knows everyone else's, and knows exactly when to use them.

"In Campo la Carrera everyone is hiding something. Cinthya is the only one who knows where to look."
06

Daniel

The only child in Campo la Carrera. He has learned to live among absences, clinging to the hope of a reunion that never comes. When he needs help, he looks for Samantha first, the only person able to make him feel like he isn't completely alone.

"Daniel has never stopped waiting. What no one dares tell him is how long a promise can last."

06 / AN EXCERPT

The beginning

CHAPTER 1

I don't remember the last time anyone asked me why I live here.

If someone did, I'd probably have to think about the answer. And that's exactly what I don't do since I arrived in Campo la Carrera. Thinking about reasons is usually the first step toward having to defend them, and no one defends anything here. Things are the way they are, or they stop being that way, without anyone needing to explain it.

That's what I like most about this place. No one asks.

In the morning I go out walking before it gets hot, while the air still holds a bit of the night, a faint coolness that never lasts long but always arrives on time. The path drops between the meadows, dips just enough to hide the village, then climbs again, as if it didn't want to let you go too far.

I walk slowly, with no destination, following a habit I don't remember choosing but that's already part of me. Sometimes I wonder how long I've been doing it. Other times I decide not to.

Here, no one walks entirely alone. Even if we're not together, I know there are others who wake at the same hour, repeat similar gestures, and share the same silence. We don't talk about it. There's no need. Over time, some people stop being just neighbors. We never agreed to it. It just happens.
