A timeless classic

Over 2,300 years of talking about strategy.

El Arte de la Guerra (The Art of War) is a unique work in the history of world literature. It began as a military treatise, but its ideas have gone far beyond war to influence economics, politics, philosophy, leadership and conflict management.

Its strength lies in its clarity: understanding the terrain, measuring the opponent, mastering timing, staying calm, and winning before the fight even begins.

Thirteen chapters of strategic wisdom.

The thirteen chapters

The architecture of strategy.

Each chapter of El Arte de la Guerra tackles a different aspect of conflict, leadership and decision-making.

01

Laying plans

The war is won before it begins.

Sun Tzu explains that every victory is born from analysis: knowing the enemy, understanding the terrain, and correctly measuring resources before acting.

02

Waging war

Conflict has a cost.

Long wars drain economies and wear people down. Speed and efficiency are strategic weapons.

03

Attack by stratagem

The best victory avoids the fight.

Overpowering an opponent without destroying them is the highest form of strategic intelligence.

04

Tactical dispositions

Preparation creates security.

A careful leader organizes their resources before conflict and keeps possible mistakes to a minimum.

05

Energy

Order creates power.

Discipline, coordination and adaptability turn a group into an unstoppable force.

06

Weak points and strong

Strike where no one expects it.

Strategy means spotting hidden weaknesses and always avoiding the enemy's strength.

07

Maneuvering

Confusion destroys armies.

Communication, logistics and emotional control are essential to maintaining cohesion.

08

Variation in tactics

Adapting is surviving.

Rigidity destroys leaders. The ability to change with circumstances creates strategic advantage.

09

The army on the march

Reading the signs changes everything.

The enemy's behavior, the environment and small movements reveal major intentions.

10

Terrain

Terrain decides battles.

Understanding physical and psychological space turns disadvantages into opportunities.

11

The nine situations

Every situation demands its own response.

There are no universal solutions. Every conflict has different rules and risks.

12

Attack by fire

Destruction must be controlled.

Sun Tzu examines how to use chaos as a tool without ever losing strategic control.

13

The use of spies

Information is power.

No strategy works without intelligence. Knowing before others changes the outcome of the conflict.
